## Overview

As a Business Requestor, the Roadmapping Tool is where you submit product requests, track their progress, and understand where your ask sits in the pipeline. You will always know the status of every request you have submitted - and why it is there.

This guide covers everything you need to submit a strong request, follow its progress, and understand how decisions get made.

---

## Getting started

When you open the tool, select **Business Requestor** from the role selector and enter your name. Your name is attached to every request you submit and is used to identify you as the original requestor on any cases you own, so use a consistent name each session.

You will land on **My Submissions** by default. This is your personal view - every request you have submitted, in every status, in one place.

---

## Submitting a request

Click **New Request** from the top of the Cases view. The submission form has the following fields.

**Request type** - Select the category that best describes your ask: Bug Report, Tooling Enhancement, or New Build. Choose the closest match and clarify in the description if needed.

**Priority** - Your self-assessed priority: Critical, High, Normal, or Low. This is your input, not the final word. The PM will score the request independently. Be honest - marking everything Critical reduces the signal value of your input over time.

**Business impact** - Describe the impact this request would have if built. Quantify where you can: how many users are affected, what it costs the business today, what improves if this is solved. Numbers are more useful than adjectives.

**Supporting links** - Add links to relevant context: customer feedback threads, data pulls, related tickets, design references, or external documentation.

**Attachments** - Upload supporting files directly to the case, up to 5MB each. Spreadsheets, screenshots, and research documents belong here rather than in a separate email or message.

Once you submit, the form closes and your request appears in My Submissions with a status of **New** and a permanent case number.

---

## Understanding your case number

Every request is assigned a case number on submission in the format `001 | Project Name`. This number is permanent and non-reusable. Even if your request is declined, its case number stays in the system forever.

Use the case number when referencing your request in conversations with PMs or in other systems. It is the fastest way to locate a specific case.

## Tracking your request

Your request moves through the following statuses. You can see the current status of every case you have submitted from My Submissions.

**New** - Your request has been received and is waiting for the PM to begin review. No action is needed from you at this stage.

**Under Review** - A PM has opened your case and is actively evaluating it. This may involve scoring, gathering more context, or waiting on a dependency.

**Roadmapped** - Your request has been scored, met the prioritization threshold, and is committed to the product roadmap. It will be built - though timing depends on where it ranks in the queue relative to other roadmapped items.

**On Hold** - Your request has merit but cannot be acted on right now. Common reasons include a dependency on another decision, a strategic timing issue, or resource constraints. On Hold is not a permanent decision. Check the case for a PM note with more context and an expected revisit timeline.

**Declined** - Your request will not be built. The PM will have documented a reason. Declined cases are never deleted from the system - you can always see your full history and the reasoning behind every decision.

## What happens after roadmapping

Once your request is roadmapped, it automatically enters the product lifecycle starting at **Discovery**. You can track its current stage in your case detail view.

**Discovery** - The team is researching and defining the problem in depth.

**Definition** - Requirements, scope, and success criteria are being documented.

**Design** - The team is designing the solution: wireframes, specifications, and design review.

**Development** - Engineering is actively building it.

**QA / Testing** - The feature is being tested before release.

**Staging** - Final validation in a staging environment before it goes live.

**Released** - It is live.

**Deprecated** - It has been retired and is no longer supported.

The PM may also flag a case as **Off-track** if it is blocked or delayed. If your roadmapped case goes Off-track, check the PM note on the case for context on the block and expected resolution.

## Writing a strong request

Requests that get roadmapped share a few things in common. Before you submit, ask yourself:

**Is the problem clear?** A PM should be able to read your description and understand exactly what is broken or missing today, without asking a follow-up question.

**Is the impact quantified?** Vague impact claims score lower. "This affects our top 20 enterprise accounts" is more actionable than "this is important to customers."

**Is the priority honest?** Requests marked Critical when they are not erode trust in your future submissions. Reserve Critical for genuine blockers.

**Is the context attached?** If there is a support thread, a data export, or a research doc that backs up your request - attach it. PMs review many requests and cannot always track down supporting evidence on your behalf.

## Commenting on your case

Each case has a comments section where you can add context, respond to PM questions, or provide updates after submission. Use comments to add new information that emerged after you submitted - a new data point, a customer quote, an escalation.

Do not resubmit a new request to add information to an existing one. Update the original case through comments.

---

## Access control

Some cases are marked restricted by the PM. If a case is restricted, you can still see it in the list view but its contents are gated. You will see a lock indicator on the case row.

You can always see your own cases in full - even restricted ones - because you are identified as the original requestor by name. If you believe you should have access to a restricted case that is not yours, contact the PM directly.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long will it take for my request to be reviewed?**

There is no guaranteed SLA in this version of the tool. If your case has been in New status for more than two weeks, reach out to the PM directly and reference your case number.

**My request was declined. Can I resubmit?**

Yes, if circumstances have changed - new data, a different business context, or a strategic shift that makes the case stronger. When you resubmit, reference the original case number in your new submission and explain what has changed. Do not resubmit the same request without new information.

**My request was put On Hold. What should I do?**

Read the PM note on your case for context and a timeline. If the hold extends past the expected revisit date, follow up with the PM directly and reference the case number.

**I submitted a request and realized I made an error.**

Add a comment to the case with the correction. Do not submit a duplicate. If the error is significant, note it in the comments and ask the PM to review before scoring.

**Can I see other people's requests?**

You can see all non-restricted cases in the All Cases view. Restricted cases show a lock indicator - you can see they exist but not their contents, unless you are the original requestor.

**Why was my high-priority request declined?**

Open the case and read the reason attached to the Declined status change. Every decline requires a documented reason. If the reason is unclear, follow up with the PM directly and reference your case number.
